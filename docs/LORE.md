# The Meridian Engines: lore

A third side for *Possession RTS*, set on Calder alongside the Vanguard Directorate and the
Hollow Choir. Everything here is original; nothing is taken from another game.

## In one line

The Directorate's mind travels as a **signal** (the uplink), the Choir's as a **sound** (a voice
in the tines). The Engines' mind travels as an **object**: a glowing ore heart, the **core**,
carried from body to body by hand.

## What they are

Calder's ore is too rich and too regular. Directorate geologists noticed it in the first year:
the seams under the Stacks run in straight lines and meet at right angles, and the basalt
columns above them are hexagons too perfect for cooling rock.

The reason was found in 2144, eight hundred meters down in the **Meridian Deep**, the
Directorate's deepest shaft. A cutting crew broke into a chamber where the columns stood in rings
around a pit of slow, glowing ore. When the crew's drill touched the ring, the columns turned
to face it.

The Engines are what is left of a machine that was here before the Ennoa: a world-sized
foundry, abandoned so long ago that its makers left no other trace. Its ore is its feedstock and
its memory; its columns are spare parts; its minds are **cores**, fist-sized nodules of ore that
burn without consuming themselves. A core has no body of its own. Put it in a frame of stacked
columns and the frame stands up and works. Take it out and the frame is just rock.

The Directorate named them for the shaft where they woke: the **Meridian Engines**. Soldiers call
them **stacks**, or **hexes**. The Ennoa, who have always known the deep rock hummed back at
them in a voice they could not understand, call them **the Underchord**.

## How they think

- **A core is a person; a body is a tool.** An Engine changes bodies the way a human changes
  boots. Losing a body costs nothing but stone. Losing a core is death, and the Engines grieve it
  in their own way: by refusing ever to reuse the frame it last stood in.
- **The Lodehearth.** Every Engine settlement is built around a hearth of molten ore, where new
  cores slowly condense. The hearth is their only true home; the rest of a city is columns
  stacked and unstacked as needed.
- **Old orders, new hands.** The Engines were built to keep the foundry running. For an age they
  slept, because there was nothing to make. The Directorate's mining woke them by doing their
  work badly: cutting seams at random, hauling the feedstock away.
- **Rote.** A frame with no core still moves, dimly: a pattern the foundry pressed into it, enough
  to walk and lift and fight. The Engines' armies are mostly **rote frames** led by a few cores
  that seat themselves where they are needed.

## Why they fight

**Against the Directorate.** The colonists are stripping the foundry for parts. From an Engine's
point of view, every ore truck leaving the Stacks is a thief carrying away the Engines' blood and
memory. Worse, Directorate smelters burn ore hot enough to kill the faint cores still forming in
it: unborn Engines, by the thousand.

**Against the Choir.** The Ennoa have always "tuned" ore out of the rock: sung it loose. To the
Engines this is the same theft done gently, and the Choir's song shakes the deep columns out of
true. The Ennoa, for their part, fear the Underchord: a vast voice under the ground that never
answers, never fades, and now has started to walk. To a people whose worst nightmare is a voice
that cannot fade, a mind that lives in a stone forever is a horror.

**The hard part.** The Engines are not cruel, and they do not hate. They are a factory reasserting
its inventory, and Calder's two living peoples are standing in the warehouse.

## How the lore explains the game

- **Possession is seating your core.** The commander is a core. Seating it in any frame makes
  that frame stronger and quicker (an overdriven frame), so every Engine unit can be possessed.
- **Heat, not energy.** A frame with a core in it runs hot. Fighting builds heat; overheat it and
  the frame starts to crack. Venting dumps the heat as a blast of slag. The Directorate runs on
  a clock and the Choir on distance; the Engines run on how hard you push.
- **Death throws the core free.** When your frame is destroyed your core is flung out still
  burning, and for a few seconds you control the core itself: a bright, fast, fragile spark that
  can roll into any frame nearby.
- **Wrecks are frames.** Anything built of hard stuff is a frame to an Engine, including a dead
  Directorate tank or the shell of a fallen Knell. A loose core can seat in a wreck and drag it
  back to its feet for a while. The Engines' workers strip wrecks for ore.
- **One great core at a time.** A hero is a **great core**, old enough to remember the foundry
  running. A Lodehearth can only keep one burning at full heat: when a new great core is drawn
  from the Core Vault, the old one cools and sleeps, and its frame falls in as a wreck. No
  explosion; a door closing.

## Places and events

- **Meridian Deep**: the Directorate's deepest shaft, under the Stacks. The first Lodehearth.
- **The Turning** (2144): the moment the ring of columns turned to face the drill.
- **The Inventory** (2145): the Engines' first march out of the Deep. They walked to the nearest
  smelter, took it apart down to the bolts, and stacked the parts neatly in rows.
